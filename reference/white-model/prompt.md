## The prompt

The source posts' author comment carries the creator's full GPT prompt
(in English, with a Chinese introduction; identical on set and reel).
The creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives in
that comment, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt asks for one separate premium design poster per photo, on a
3:4 canvas in two equal halves. The top half preserves the original
photograph with only subtle sophisticated grading. The bottom half
interprets the subject's most essential identity, structure,
proportions and spatial character and reconstructs it as a small-scale
conceptual model / sculptural form study — massing, cutting, layering,
cantilevering, recesses, projections, solid-void relationships — pure,
condensed, immediately recognizable. The subject sits small in
extremely generous negative space. The palette is near-monochromatic
white / ivory / charcoal (value over hue), with the background chosen
per the photo's atmosphere (pure black, deep charcoal, or extremely
pale near-white) and ONE tiny accent of the photo's signature color
only when symbolically important. Lighting is restrained and precise.
The feel is an architectural massing study / conceptual form study —
never a photorealistic 3D or commercial product render. Typography is
extremely minimal: a few words, small, widely letter-spaced.

> Full prompt text: see the source posts' author comment (linked above).

## English translation of the introduction

The caption (Traditional Chinese) shares what photos turned into
"white models" look like — a cool, delicate kind of photo conversion.
The top half keeps the original photo; the bottom half keeps deleting:
background removed, color removed, unimportant details all removed. In
the end only the most recognizable outlines, poses and proportions
remain, rebuilt into a small white conceptual model — some places cut
open or hollowed out, some stacked or floating, with much space
deliberately left around. Although so much has been deleted, you can
still tell at a glance which photo it came from. People, buildings,
pets, plants and cars all work; photos with clearer outlines turn out
better. Readers are invited to copy the prompt from the images / first
comment.
