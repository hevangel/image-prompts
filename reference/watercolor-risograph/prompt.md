## The prompt

The full prompt sits in the post caption and identically in the author's first comment on the set post. The creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives there, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt turns each uploaded photograph, separately and in upload order, into its own independent premium "Original Photography × Abstract Watercolor × Bold Risograph" comparison poster on a strict 3:4 portrait canvas, split horizontally into exactly two equal 50% sections joined seamlessly at the midpoint — no side-by-side layout, extra panels, divider lines, borders, text, labels, logos, arrows, or watermarks:

- **Top half — original photography.** A faithful copy of the original photo — identity, face, expression, pose, clothing, proportions, objects, perspective, composition, lighting, textures and original color — with only extremely subtle editorial grading. Minimal crop or natural background extension only; no stretching, distorting, repainting, replacing, or altering subjects; no watercolor/print effects.
- **Bottom half — abstract watercolor × bold risograph.** A contemporary limited-edition fine-art print (wet-on-wet watercolor, bold risograph, screen-printing, minimalist negative space). Identify the photo's 2–5 most recognizable features, preserve their proportions, positions and relationships, eliminate 80–90% of unnecessary detail, and rebuild them with 2–5 interconnected groups of oversized loose expressive watercolor washes (flowing pigment, translucent bleeding, organic diffusion, irregular edges, pigment pooling, spontaneous brushwork). No literal tracing or detailed reproduction.
- **Risograph texture.** Clearly visible halftone dots, fine and coarse ink grain, irregular ink deposits, stencil imperfections, bold semi-transparent spot-color overlays inside selected washes; overprinted color interactions, ink-density variation, controlled registration misalignment along secondary color boundaries; soft diffusion mostly, sharper printed edges in places. Offsets must never distort faces, hands, key objects, or structural relationships.
- **Color.** Extract 2–4 representative colors exclusively from the current photo, then intensify saturation, contrast, and color separation: one dominant color plus supporting colors and limited accents; overlapping translucent inks create extra hues naturally. No arbitrary neon colors, fixed palettes, or unrelated decorative tones.
- **Negative space.** Genuinely pure white (#FFFFFF) background, about 55–75% of the lower panel remaining visually unprinted; the most saturated colors, strongest halftone, and registration offsets concentrated in 1–2 focal areas; secondary forms dissolve into white; grain/halftone confined to painted/printed regions, never across the whole background.
- **Subject adaptation.** Per-type rules: portraits (head direction, expression, hairstyle, pose, gestures); action/relationships (movement, interaction, contact points); architecture/streets (building masses, perspective, road direction, spatial rhythm); landscapes (mountain contours, horizons, vegetation, atmospheric layers); animals/flowers/still life (silhouettes, proportions, color relationships).
- **Prohibitions.** No watercolor photo filters, realistic tracing, black outlines, detailed linework, vector-style illustration, cartoon effects, heavy opaque painting, random abstract shapes, invented subjects, excessive decoration, full-background textures, aged or yellowed paper, uncontrolled color shifts, 3D ghosting, glitch effects, or excessive halftone coverage. No poster titles.
- **Output/behavior.** Upper half stays a faithful refined photograph; lower half becomes a bold, expressive, recognizable abstract watercolor/risograph work with dramatic color contrast, visible print grain, layered transparent inks, handmade imperfections, and generous white negative space — striking from a distance, recognizable at medium range, richly textured up close. Closing imperatives: preserve the photograph, abstract the subject, amplify the colors, reveal the ink, celebrate controlled misregistration, protect the negative space. Generate the finished poster directly, with no questions, explanations, or additional text.

## English translation of the introduction

So this is what a photo looks like when watercolor bleed meets a little print grain! Sharing another fun new way to play with photos today 😊 This time it's watercolor and retro print!

Have you ever wondered what happens when watercolor colors bloom and spread, and then you add fine print grain on top?

This time I'm putting those two feelings together! Watercolor colors are like pigment flowing into water — slowly spreading out, overlapping each other, edges a little blurry and never quite regular.

But look closely: hidden inside those blooming colors are tiny little grains. Some places the color is stronger, some places it's faint, and there's even a slight misalignment here and there.

I really like this feeling — especially how watercolor's originally soft colors, once they meet print grain, suddenly gain a rough texture; from far away it's watercolor, but up close there are fine print traces hiding in it.

Watercolor colors don't have to line up perfectly, and the picture doesn't have to be perfectly clear — at times like these, those slightly misaligned colors and grains are the most beautiful part 😁

Now it's your turn to find a favorite photo and try this watercolor-plus-retro-print feeling! 😊
