## The prompt

The full prompt sits in the author's first comment on the set post. The creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives there, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt turns each uploaded photograph, separately and in upload order (never mixing subjects or elements between photos), into its own independent "Original Photography × Minimalist Fine-Line Illustration" comparison poster on a strict vertical 3:4 canvas, split horizontally into two seamless equal 50% sections — no borders, text, labels, logos or watermarks:

- **Top half — original photography.** A faithful copy of the original photo — identity, features, proportions, pose, composition, perspective, textures, lighting and colours — with only minimal cropping or natural background extension.
- **Bottom half — minimalist fine-line illustration.** Only the single most recognisable primary subject is isolated; background, clutter and secondary elements are removed, and the subject is redrawn as an elegant ultra-minimalist fine-line illustration in precise thin black/charcoal contours, with an accurate silhouette, simplified structural detail and subtle line-weight variation, in a Scandinavian editorial / architectural-drafting aesthetic. Orientation, proportions and defining features are preserved, and the visual connection to the original stays clear.
- **Colours.** At most two authentic colours extracted from the subject, used as very restrained flat accents; 80–90% of the illustration is neutral linework and negative space. Accents are omitted when unnecessary.
- **Layout.** A clean warm ivory/cream background, optionally with a subtle paper texture; at least 50% negative space (ideally 55–70%), with the subject compact and visually centred, occupying roughly 25–40% of the lower section.
- **Avoid.** Invented objects, decorative symbols, unnecessary scenery, heavy shading, gradients, 3D effects, rough sketches or excessive detail.
- **Subtraction idea.** Architecture keeps its most distinctive roof, windows and outline; people keep hairstyle, clothing outline and familiar pose; pets keep ears, body shape and most recognisable action. The style adapts to architecture, people, animals, vehicles, objects and landscapes.

## English translation of the introduction

The caption (Traditional Chinese) reads:

「手機裡捨不得刪的照片，試着換一種方式留下來，有時候畫面留白越多，越容易注意到重要的東西。這次想分享一種很刻意拿掉多餘背景，只留下主角的細緻輪廓，讓普通照片也能嘗試呈現極簡感。這次的風格是「原始攝影 × 極簡細線插畫」。�

"Photos in my phone that I can't bear to delete — try keeping them in a different way. Sometimes, the more blank space in a picture, the easier it is to notice what matters. This time I want to share a style that deliberately removes excess background, leaving only the subject's fine outline, so ordinary photos can also try for a minimalist feel. This style is 'Original Photography × Minimalist Fine-Line Illustration'."

## How this relates to neighboring entries

Filed as its own entry: it joins the comparison-poster family (see `comparison-poster`, `fresh-illustration`, `color-block-breakout`, `japanese-retro-manga`, `abstract-gouache`, `neon-thick-impasto`, `japanese-hand-drawn`, `translucent-tracing-paper`, `white-space-illustration`, `black-white-drawing`, `european-travel-film`, `reflection-protagonist`, `minimalist-simplification`, `cracked-palette-knife`, `paper-cut-fairytale`, `rice-paper-ink`, `watercolor-risograph`, `naive-risograph`, `poetry-of-moments`, `picasso-cubist`, `three-color-linocut` and `crayon-hand-drawn`) as its fine-line branch — the lower half is a precise thin-contour line drawing of one subject on a cream ground with at least 50% negative space and at most two restrained accent colours, and it carries no poster titles. It is distinct from `fine-line-poster` (Oct 5, fragile broken fine lines, a tiny 10–25% subject in vast warm-white space, and one handwritten phrase per poster), from `one-line-contour` (Oct 9, minimal continuous contours with exactly one spot colour and the subject occupying about half the panel), from `minimalist-simplification` (Nov 6, flat-vector illustration in up to four colours with the subject at 55–65% of the lower panel), and from `comparison-poster` (Aug 31, the family's original comparison-poster entry, whose lower halves are fuller illustrations rather than single-subject line drawings).
