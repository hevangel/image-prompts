## The prompt

The full prompt sits in the post caption and identically in the author's first comment on the set post. The creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives there, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt turns each uploaded photograph, separately and in upload order, into its own independent "Poetry of Moments" photography × vintage geometric illustration comparison poster on a strict 3:4 portrait canvas, split horizontally into exactly two equal 50% halves, seamlessly joined — no borders, dividers, frames or extra panels:

- **Top half — original photography.** A faithful copy of the original photo — identity, face, expression, pose, clothing, objects, composition, perspective, natural light, texture and authentic colour — with only subtle exposure adjustment, proportional cropping and natural background extension. Never change subjects, invent objects, or apply illustration effects to the top half.
- **Bottom half — vintage geometric illustration.** The same complete scene redrawn as a minimalist vintage geometric illustration, keeping the subjects, poses, spatial relationships, composition and emotional atmosphere, simplified to 3–5 defining features using circles, semicircles, smooth curves, soft triangles and organic shapes. Avoid rigid grids, mechanical block collages, excessive abstraction and unnecessary details.
- **Style.** Vintage Geometric Illustration × Minimal Risograph × Modern Editorial Art: 3–5 harmonious colours extracted from the original, muted retro tones, gentle contrast, warm ivory paper, subtle paper fibres, fine risograph grain and extremely slight print misregistration, with clean balanced refined shapes and generous natural negative space.
- **Typography.** Exactly one medium-sized elegant Didot/Bodoni serif English title of 1–4 words, relevant to the subject or mood, placed asymmetrically in an unobtrusive empty area of the lower half without covering important subjects. No subtitles, captions, dates, numbers, logos, watermarks or additional text — while preserving any text already visible in the original photo.
- **Finish.** Clear hierarchy, recognisable scene correspondence, restrained geometric forms, soft colours and comfortable whitespace; avoid decorative clutter, excessive textures and oversized typography. Strong contrast between authentic photography and poetic illustration while preserving the original visual narrative. One clean, elegant, high-resolution editorial poster per photo.

## English translation of the introduction

Some photos are more beautiful when kept simpler. This time I want to share a very comfortable photo-transformation style: keep the photo's original sense of reality, then use simple geometric shapes and soft retro colours to redraw a familiar photo poetically. The lower half turns people, architecture, light and shadow, and scenery into simple circles, arcs and geometric colour blocks, with not too many complex lines and no overly vivid colours. It takes only a few retro colours from the original photo, plus paper grain, blank space and a simple English title, so the whole thing looks like a work in a vintage art magazine. Although many details are removed, the original photo's atmosphere remains. Whether travel scenery, street-corner architecture, or a casual everyday snapshot, all suit being reworked in this style. Maybe a familiar photo on your phone can also become a work worth collecting.

(Note: the post's third line reads "保留照片原本的真實候，" — with the character 候, apparently a typo for 感, "sense of reality"; translated here as written, i.e. as "sense of reality".)

Filed as its own entry: it joins the comparison-poster family (see `comparison-poster`, `fresh-illustration`, `color-block-breakout`, `japanese-retro-manga`, `abstract-gouache`, `neon-thick-impasto`, `japanese-hand-drawn`, `translucent-tracing-paper`, `white-space-illustration`, `black-white-drawing`, `european-travel-film`, `reflection-protagonist`, `minimalist-simplification`, `cracked-palette-knife`, `paper-cut-fairytale`, `rice-paper-ink`, `watercolor-risograph` and `naive-risograph`) as its vintage-geometric-illustration branch — unlike the family's painted, graded, illustrated, ink-wash, watercolor or crayon branches, the lower half here is a restrained geometric illustration built from circles, arcs and soft shapes in 3–5 muted retro colours on warm ivory paper, and it is the family's only branch so far whose posters carry a printed serif title. It is distinct from `naive-risograph` (Nov 11, crayon colour blocks with wobbly outlines on a cream ground, no titles) and from `watercolor-risograph` (Nov 10, abstract watercolor washes with halftone on pure white, no titles).
