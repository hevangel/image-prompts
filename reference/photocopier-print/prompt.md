## The prompt

The source post's author comment carries the creator's full GPT prompt
(written in Chinese, with a Chinese introduction). The creator
publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives in that
comment, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt asks for each uploaded photo to become its own independent
high-end design poster, never splicing photos, on a 3:4 canvas in two
zones of exactly equal height. The top zone preserves the original
photo faithfully with only fine, restrained high-end grading; the
environment may extend naturally and the subject is never stretched,
distorted or changed. The bottom zone extracts only the most
recognizable subject, contour, structure, pose and narrative
relations and reconstructs them as a two-spot-color, misregistration-
flavored hand-drawn illustration / Risograph-style editorial
illustration. The whole photo is not redrawn; most background and
secondary information is actively deleted. The bottom half gets
independent art direction — bold deletion, rearrangement, cropping,
scaling, repositioning and white space; the subject may be off-center,
edge-touching, floating or partially cropped; one clear visual focus;
deletion is prioritized over filling. A small, stamp-like subject sits
in a very large amount of white space — white space is a compositional
element equal to the subject (positive/negative shapes, density
contrast, directionality, asymmetric balance); the whole bottom half
stays quiet and open with lingering resonance. The two-spot-color
system is strict: one color carries color areas, mass and partial
shadow; the other carries contours, structure and emphasis; the two
most expressive color relationships are chosen from the photo rather
than the objects' real colors. Color blocks may be incomplete;
contours and color areas may detach; slight registration offset,
uneven ink density, ink breaks, grain, and paper ink-absorption/fiber
texture are kept for a real paper Risograph/silkscreen feel. Text is
minimal editorial intervention only, language unrestricted: a
distilled word or short phrase placed quietly in the white space; no
fixed title template, no information pile-up. Core principle: don't
draw the photo again — understand it, delete most of it, then direct
it into a more convincing work.

> Full prompt text: see the source post's author comment (linked above).

## English translation of the introduction

The caption (Traditional Chinese) shares a photocopier-print poster
instruction in a style he has really liked lately. The method is
simple: the top half keeps the original photo for comparison; the
bottom half has AI re-analyze the same photo into a printmaking
poster like photocopying, color registration, digital printing. The
interesting part is the AI's understanding of the photo — what is most
worth presenting, which contour is most recognizable — after which it
deletes most of the background, keeps only a small core, and works
with two main colors, simple lines, large white space, and a little of
the offset, grain and uneven ink of photocopier overprinting. What he
likes most: it keeps simplifying — smaller subject, emptier frame,
lines and color blocks deliberately not perfectly aligned — and those
imperfections give the photo more feeling. Above, the photo records
the moment; below re-organizes what is worth remembering. The full
instruction is in the images / first comment.
