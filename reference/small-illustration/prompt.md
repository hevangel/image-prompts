## The prompt

The set post's author comment carries the creator's full GPT prompt
(written in Chinese, with a Chinese introduction; the Oct 16 reel's
author comment carries an English version of the same system). The
creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives in
those comments, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt asks for one independent premium editorial poster per
photo, on a strict 3:4 canvas in two equal halves. The top half
preserves the original photograph — photographic, not illustrated —
with only subtle high-end grading. The bottom half extracts only the
most recognizable subjects, contours, poses and narrative
relationships and reinterprets them as a small-scale editorial spot
illustration, aggressively eliminating secondary detail and
background. Its defining principle: an exceptionally small
illustration surrounded by an enormous amount of untouched paper —
the drawing occupies only a small portion of its half, placed by the
subject's natural direction and weight, with no background, panel,
border or frame, and the paper itself as an active compositional
element; when in doubt, remove more. People, buildings, animals,
plants, objects and landscapes may be boldly generalized, cropped,
omitted or recombined, but the correspondence with the photo above
must read at a glance. The drawing uses restrained gestural strokes
in rough, dry media — wax crayon, oil pastel, coarse graphite, China
marker — a few powerful strokes, simplified shapes, broken contours,
scuffs, incomplete coverage, exposed paper and slight misalignment:
tactile, handmade, slightly imperfect. Color is strictly three
elements: warm gray-white paper, charcoal black carrying the
structure, contours and main visual weight, and rust red / brick red
used only as a sparse selective accent at a few visual jump-points —
distributed by the picture's rhythm, not matched to objects' real
colors. Typography is minimal and optional — a few quiet words in
charcoal or rust red in the negative space, small and restrained,
omitted if they don't help. Excluded: complete scene reconstruction,
filled backgrounds, photorealistic redrawing, polished vector looks,
multicolored rendering, complex shadows, cartoon or 3D feel,
oversized headlines, templated poster feel.

> Full prompt text: see the source posts' author comments (linked above).

## English translation of the introduction

The captions (Traditional Chinese) present a "deliberately draw less"
small-illustration style. The top half keeps the original photo for
comparison; the bottom half does not redraw the photo but deletes most
of its information, keeping only the most recognizable outlines, poses
and a few key features, shrunk into a tiny small illustration. The
beauty is in what is not drawn — a small figure surrounded by a large
warm-white paper area, no filled-in background or packed color blocks,
lines allowed to break, skew, leave white and show scratch marks, like
crayon, oil pastel or 2B pencil left directly on paper. Colors are
deliberately restricted: charcoal black carries the main structure,
rust red serves only a few visual accents, and the warm gray-white
paper itself is the third color. The result feels like a magazine, an
indie publication or a cultural-event poster. The Oct 16 reel presents
the same system as a fine-tuned variant of this earlier posting.
