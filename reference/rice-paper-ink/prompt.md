## The prompt

The full prompt sits in the post caption and identically in the author's first comment on the set post. The creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives there, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt turns each uploaded photograph, separately and never merged, into a premium "ORIGINAL × REIMAGINED" comparison poster in traditional Chinese ink wash (guohua), on a strict 3:4 portrait canvas split horizontally into two exactly equal halves with a thin off-white dividing line:

- **Layout.** Top half, labeled "ORIGINAL": a faithful copy of the original photo — same subjects, faces, expressions, poses, clothing, proportions, architecture, objects, lighting, textures and colors, with no cropping, reframing, retouching, filters or color changes. Bottom half, labeled "REIMAGINED": the ink-wash reinterpretation.
- **Bottom half — transformation.** Traditional Chinese ink-wash painting on aged rice paper — visible paper fibers and texture, lightly yellowed and slightly uneven surface, faint deckled edge. Ink in graded black washes from light grey to deep black, dry-brush texture, soft bleeding at the edges, flowing expressive strokes. Keep the original's exact composition, main subject, pose, framing and structure; the subject must stay clearly recognizable, and the two halves must depict the same scene and layout.
- **Simplification.** Reduce the scene to a few large ink masses; leave large areas of unpainted paper (negative space), especially around the edges and corners; do not fill the whole sheet; avoid busy detail. The most flavorful part is not drawing every detail clearly — a few bold strokes, faint washes, and a large blank area.
- **Color.** Essentially monochrome ink on cream rice paper, with exactly ONE restrained vermilion-red accent, taken from a red element already present in the original photo (same position, size, shape and color). In photos with no red, add one small vermilion accent object of the generator's choice (a seal, a flower, a lantern, or an umbrella).
- **Title.** A short English title (2–4 words, uppercase serif) centered at the very bottom of the poster, inside the bottom half, naming the subject or theme (e.g. "THE SEA"). It must be a real, short, appropriate title, not random letters.
- **Prohibitions.** No frame, no seal or stamp marks anywhere, no watermark, no logo, no border, no extra text, no added objects, and no cropping of the original.
- **Output/behavior.** One single high-resolution 3:4 image per uploaded photo.

> Full prompt text: see the source post caption / author's first comment (linked above).

**Note on the seals:** the prompt forbids seals, yet most of the archived posters carry a small red seal stamp at the bottom right (all but #9 "THE CAMERA"; #5 and #6 are cropped below the fold in the viewer, so theirs is unconfirmed). A commenter asked whether the prompt always leaves red unprocessed; the author replied at 7:00 AM on Nov 9 that the vermilion red is intentional (pure black-and-white ink felt monotonous, and borrowing a color from the original sometimes clashed), but that the small seal at the bottom right had not been handled — and the post was updated. Photos 4, 5, 6 and 9 show a later "2h" timestamp in the viewer while the others show "4h", consistent with that update; the seal is still visible on most posters, so the update did not remove it.

Everyday photos can become elegant too 🎨 You don't need to understand Chinese painting, and you don't need to know how to hold a brush. With this prompt, you can turn everyday photos into ink-wash works full of Eastern charm 😊 This time I'm using my favorite top-and-bottom comparison poster again, with the lower half rendered in traditional Chinese ink wash.
