## The prompt

The full prompt sits in the set caption and in the reel's author comment. The creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives there, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt turns each uploaded photograph into its own vertical comparison poster, handling photos independently and in upload order — never collaging or mixing images, colors, subjects or compositions between photos:

- **Layout.** A premium full-bleed 3:4 vertical editorial comparison poster in exactly two equal horizontal halves (50/50), with no borders, dividers, labels or text.
- **Upper half.** Stays faithful to the original — identity, facial features, expression, hairstyle, clothing, pose, important objects, natural color, lighting and atmosphere — with only minimal cropping to fit, and no stylization or reconstruction.
- **Lower half — medium and color.** A playful, minimalist hand-inked black-and-white illustration using only pure white for background and negative space and near-black for bold outlines and solid shapes.
- **Lower half — line quality.** Broad, blunt, irregular marker- or loaded-brush strokes with visible hand-drawn wobble, pressure variation, imperfect curves and organic closed contours; thin, smooth, vector-clean lines are excluded.
- **Lower half — figures.** People are simplified into expressive characters with large irregular rounded heads, small dot or curved eyes, simple ring-shaped noses, friendly expressions, mitten-like hands and chunky limbs, while keeping recognizable hairstyles, glasses, accessories, poses, actions and interactions; non-human subjects get the same playful simplification.
- **Lower half — reduction.** The primary subject, visual story, important objects and relationships are kept, but roughly 70–90% of unnecessary background detail is removed; the composition stays flat and graphic with generous clean white negative space and bold black silhouettes. A small dense irregular dot pattern may be used on at most one garment or textile area, and is omitted if none suits.
- **Exclusions.** No invented characters, objects or activities; no photorealism in the illustration, 3D, gradients, gray tones, shadows, realistic shading, extra colors, excessive detail, corporate-icon look, mechanical symmetry, all-over grunge or malformed anatomy; and no text, letters, numbers, logos, signatures, watermarks, decorative frames or extra panels anywhere.

## English translation of the introduction

This time, sharing a way to turn photos into a warm black-and-white hand-drawn style. The introduction goes on to say that the first image is a story-rich street-musician scene — guitar, passers-by, warm overhead lights — and that the lower half keeps only the memorable elements (the musician's action and expression, hat, glasses and coat features, guitar, drum and bags, and the distant crowd and street-light atmosphere) redrawn in simple, direct black-and-white lines. It describes the slightly imperfect feel as like strokes made one by one with a marker or brush: not perfect, a little casual, which gives the picture more character and flavor. It says photos with people, action, street scenes, travel feeling or everyday life suit the style, and invites readers to try the prompt with their own photo and post their results.

## How this relates to neighboring entries

Filed as its own entry, not folded into `black-line-drawing` or `japanese-hand-drawn`: all three are black-line styles, but this one is a playful, characterful marker/brush illustration in pure black on pure white — large rounded heads, dot eyes, mitten hands, chunky limbs — with a warm, spontaneous, handcrafted editorial character, while `black-line-drawing` and `japanese-hand-drawn` use finer, more restrained linework. It joins the comparison-poster family (see `comparison-poster`, `fresh-illustration`, `color-block-breakout`, `japanese-retro-manga`, `abstract-gouache`, `neon-thick-impasto`, `japanese-hand-drawn`, `translucent-tracing-paper` and `white-space-illustration`) as its warm black-and-white branch. Source photos are credited in the caption to Pexels.
