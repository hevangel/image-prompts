## The prompt

The full prompt sits in the author's comment on both the set post and the reel. The creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives in that comment, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt turns each uploaded photograph into its own vertical comparison poster, handling photos independently — never mixing images, colors, subjects or compositions between photos:

- **Layout.** A strict 3:4 portrait canvas in exactly two equal horizontal halves: the original photograph above, the tracing-paper interpretation below, with no dividers, borders, labels, logos or watermarks.
- **Upper half.** Stays faithful to the original — identities, faces, proportions and gestures preserved, with only minimal exposure or color adjustment.
- **Lower half — simplification.** Only 2–5 essential visual memory cues are kept; about 75–90% of the secondary background is removed, and about 55–70% of the lower panel stays visually quiet, with a compact focal grouping and generous negative space.
- **Lower half — paper layering.** The subject is rendered in about 3–5 thin, semi-transparent, matte, softly milky tracing-paper layers with fine fibers, at three clarity levels: one gesture through a single layer; principal forms softened through two overlapping layers; secondary forms partly obscured under more layers. Softness comes from translucency and layered occlusion, not blur.
- **Lower half — color and light.** Colors are derived independently per photograph from its own palette over a clean off-white or very lightly source-tinted background, avoiding yellowing, sepia or aged-paper tones. Lighting follows the source, with broad soft light through the sheets and restrained translucent luminosity.
- **What it avoids.** Realistic rendering, dense outlines, polygonal facets, watercolor blooms, heavy grain, glossy plastic, acrylic, frosted glass, thick cardboard, origami and paper-cut looks.
- **Text rules.** An optional 2–5 word English caption in the lower panel's negative space, in quiet editorial typography in a subtle source-palette color (allowed to stay crisp as a top printed layer); no subtitles, decorative microcopy, or invented places, dates, years or numbers. A fully text-free result is equally acceptable.

## English translation of the introduction

Have you ever seen a Japanese-style paper sliding door? This time, the prompt shared has that translucent, hazy, soft feel. It is Shoji (障子). The method reworks a photo using sheets of translucent tracing paper. The special thing is the "paper transparency layering": in some places it looks like only one thin sheet of paper, with the shapes and colors underneath still clearly visible; in other places, where sheets overlap layer by layer, colors gradually fade and lines become softer and hazier. It does not simply blur the photo; through paper of different transparency, the original subject, light, and color reappear faintly, half-hidden. Depicting the same photo another way makes the whole picture calmer and softer, even with a feeling like a memory. Because each photo's own light and color differ, the effect of the overlapping paper will feel different too. Example photos are credited to Pexels, and the full GPT prompt is in the first comment, ready to copy.

## How this relates to neighboring entries

Filed as its own entry, not folded into `frosted-glass`: both use translucency, but frosted glass reinterprets the scene as a refined minimalist abstraction of geometric forms, flat color fields and selective region-specific blur on an ivory ground, while this style keeps the scene's own forms and re-renders them through layered semi-transparent tracing-paper sheets, with softness from occlusion rather than blur. It joins the comparison-poster family (see `comparison-poster`, `fresh-illustration`, `color-block-breakout`, `japanese-retro-manga`, `abstract-gouache`, `neon-thick-impasto` and `japanese-hand-drawn`) as its translucent-paper branch.
