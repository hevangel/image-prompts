## The prompt

The source post's author comment carries the creator's full GPT prompt
— a **templated** prompt (in Chinese, with a Chinese introduction).
The creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives in
that comment, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The template's fill-in variables are: Brand [品牌名稱], Food
[食物／飲品], Headline [2–4 Chinese characters], Physical Interaction
[纏繞 winding / 斷裂 breaking / 滴流 dripping / 剝離 peeling], Accent
Color [顏色], Product Name [產品名稱], Price [價格]. The output is a
9:16 premium editorial food poster in which real food physically
interacts with oversized Chinese characters and directly changes their
structure. Background: warm cream or ivory. Headline: heavy, strongly
geometric black Chinese type. The food must be realistic, natural,
with true photographic texture. Overall: abundant white space,
asymmetric composition, grid-breaking editorial layout fusing premium
European F&B branding with contemporary Chinese magazine design. The
food must not merely sit in front of or behind the characters — food
and characters are objects in one real physical space, and the chosen
interaction must clearly alter strokes, structure or material
behavior: winding → strands wrap around, pass through and pull
specific strokes tight, with slight believable tension deformation at
contact points; breaking → the food cracks along a clear force
direction and the characters fracture, displace and structurally
offset along the same axis; dripping → real liquid / sauce / honey /
cheese / drink flows down the strokes under gravity, gradually
covering or replacing parts of the geometric strokes; peeling → the
character surface has real thickness and is physically lifted to
reveal a different food layer beneath, the lifted part remaining
attached and curling naturally. Only one primary physical mechanism
per poster; every local deformation must follow real contact, weight,
tension, gravity, viscosity or fracture logic; strokes untouched by
the food stay clean, sharp, geometric and highly readable. Random
liquefaction, purely decorative overlap, generic 3D type effects and
over-digital distortion are avoided. Layout: strong scale contrast,
asymmetry, large negative space; a little meaningful small information
is allowed (ingredients, opening hours, address) but no traditional
menu layout. Headline in heavy black; product name and price may pair
with high-contrast Song type or italic serif. Color stays minimal:
cream ground, black type, the food's natural colors, and one accent
color. The result must read instantly even at thumbnail size — food
and the giant headline first, then how the food truly changes the
characters — and must reproduce the specified Chinese characters
exactly: no wrong, mutated or meaningless characters, with high
recognizability and legibility.

> Full prompt text: see the source post's author comment (linked above).

## English translation of the introduction

The caption (Traditional Chinese) says he found a fun food-poster
prompt: put the food together with oversized characters — noodles can
wrap around the strokes, sauce can drip down the characters,
ingredients can crack the characters, you can even peel the
characters' surface open. The point: this time the photo isn't simply
stacked on the text; the food's weight, tension, flow and breakage
really affect the characters' structure. He organizes four ready-to-use
interaction modes: (1) 纏繞 winding — noodles, candy and long
ingredients pass through and wrap around the strokes; (2) 斷裂
breaking — when cookies, chocolate or fried food cracks, the
characters crack along the same direction; (3) 滴流 dripping —
coffee, honey, sauce or cheese flows along the strokes and gradually
replaces part of the characters; (4) 剝離 peeling — treat the
characters as having a real "skin" that can be lifted to reveal the
food underneath. Add a cream background, generous white space, heavy
black type and a little brand information and it looks like a
restaurant brand poster. It works well beyond snacks — coffee, ramen,
donuts, chocolate, pizza, ice cream, drinks — just change brand name +
food + a 2–4-character headline + interaction mode + main color +
product name + price to get a completely different version. The full
instruction is in the images / first comment.
