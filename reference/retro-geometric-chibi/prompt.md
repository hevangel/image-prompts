## The prompt

The full prompt sits in the post body (a long detailed version, under "完整指令") and, in a shorter condensed form, in the author's first comment on the set post (under "GPT指令："). The creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives there, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt turns each uploaded photograph independently into its own "Crayon Little World V1.3 — Retro Geometric Deadpan Chibi Edition" comparison poster on one strict vertical 3:4 canvas, split horizontally into two perfectly equal 50% sections, seamlessly joined, with no borders, dividers or extra panels:

- **Top half — original photography.** A faithful copy of the original photo — all important subjects, faces, hairstyles, clothing, poses, animals, objects, composition, perspective, natural lighting and authentic colours — with only subtle colour grading and minimal cropping or background extension allowed. Do not redraw, distort, mirror or remove important subjects.
- **Bottom half — retro geometric crayon illustration.** A small, awkward retro geometric collage illustration on clean warm ivory paper with subtle grain and imperfect printing texture and generous negative space. Extract 2–4 muted vintage colours from the photo. Take one large, soft, pale, irregular silhouette inspired by the most recognisable subject; the illustrated composition occupies only about 35–45% of the bottom section, the rest mostly empty.
- **Characters.** Recognisable oversized-head, short-bodied, slightly asymmetrical 2–3-head-tall chibi characters built from rough hand-cut polygons, trapezoids, triangles and semicircles — overlapping paper shapes with broken charcoal-black crayon outlines.
- **Facial features (marked "MOST IMPORTANT").** Build the actual features from irregular geometric paper-cut pieces, about 70% recognisable structure and 30% playful displacement: uneven half-closed eyes, mismatched geometric eyelids, short crooked eyebrows, offset triangular noses, tiny asymmetric mouths, subtle geometric cheeks. Deadpan, tired, mildly unimpressed expressions; no exaggerated emotion. Preserve each subject's defining features, species, clothing, pose, accessories and relationships — otherwise the characters become unrecognisable.
- **Texture.** Rough wax-crayon grain, imperfect outlines, misregistered colour blocks, matte vintage fills, handmade collage texture — not clean vector.
- **Objects.** Where appropriate, enlarge one authentic object from the source photo to 1.5–2.5× the character's size for gentle absurdity; no invented narrative objects. At most three tiny abstract geometric accents.
- **Slogan.** One original 8–16-character Traditional Chinese micro-poem, gentle, innocent and slightly world-weary in humour, in 2–3 small handwritten lines in the bottom section's negative space. Prioritise correct Taiwanese Traditional Chinese characters; omit the text if accuracy cannot be guaranteed.
- **Forbidden.** No reproducing logos or signage, no complete scene duplication, no excessive abstraction, no identical character expressions, no facial features on inanimate objects, no glossy rendering, anime eyes, neon colours, crowded backgrounds, extra subjects, invented scenery, English subtitles, unnecessary typography, watermarks or additional panels. The closing image: a beautifully imperfect page from a vintage independent children's picture book.

## English translation of the introduction

The caption (Traditional Chinese) opens by asking: draw the faces in a photo looking utterly world-weary — so why does it come out even cuter? They clearly can't be bothered with anyone, yet you can't help looking twice. This time every leading character in the photos becomes a group of retro crayon little people with geometric features, too lazy to perform. The same photo can somehow express two completely different attitudes to life: turn the people into big heads, short little limbs, crooked features, and a face that can't be bothered with anyone. They look helpless, yet inexplicably adorable — eyes a little crooked somehow have even more character. The most special thing this time is the facial features: not the usual round, bright-eyed Q-version characters, but eyes, eyebrows, nose and mouth all taken apart into irregular geometric coloured paper, then pieced back onto the face one by one. One eye higher than the other, eyebrows crooked, nose off to one side, even the corners of the mouth too lazy to line up — plus a pair of half-closed eyes. The whole face seems to say: "I'm not unhappy, I just don't want to go to work today." But the funny thing is, crooked as the features are, each person's original characteristics still have to be kept — otherwise, if they're so crooked even they can't recognise themselves, that really would be too world-weary. The characters are world-weary enough already, and life still has to keep making things hard for them. Add one short world-weary little poem, as if secretly writing down that character's inner thoughts for the day. Calm years above, collective spacing-out below: the top half keeps the original photo, recording life as it really is, while the bottom half lets the AI redraw it as a retro geometric crayon illustration. Only after finishing did I realise: sometimes features don't have to be so proper, and life doesn't have to stay perfect all the time — a little crooked can be very cute too.
