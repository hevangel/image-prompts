- **Distinctions:** a sibling of `lingering-afterglow` （餘韻） in the two-zone template family, but drawn in black hand lines with a light watercolor wash on a xuan-paper ground with an ice-blue accent — the afterglow style uses a single faint cool-monochrome line system on warm gray-beige aged paper. Also distinct from `fine-line-poster` (Oct 5) and `rough-brushed-lines` (Oct 14).

## The prompt

The companion reel's author comment carries the creator's full GPT
prompt (written in Chinese, with a Chinese introduction; the set
post's caption matches the reel's). The creator publishes it for
followers to copy. The full text lives in that comment, linked above;
what follows is a structural summary.

Another member of the two-zone template family: each uploaded photo
becomes its own independent high-end design poster, never spliced, on
a 3:4 canvas in two zones strictly 1:1. The top zone preserves the
original photo faithfully with only slight high-end grading. The
bottom zone does not redraw the photo — it distills only the most
touching, story-worthy fragment and shrinks it into a very small
whitespace illustration drawn in black hand-drawn lines with a light
watercolor wash, on a xuan-paper-textured ground. The palette is
essentially rice-paper white + black, with a single accent of light
ice-blue sampled from the photo above. Everything else is heavily
deleted; the composition is a tiny subject in large white space, with
minimal text at the edges.

> Full prompt text: see the source reel's author comment (linked above).

## English translation of the introduction

The caption (Traditional Chinese) explains that the AI does not redraw
the whole photo; it keeps only the most story-worthy part, and that
part stays small. The top half remains the original photo (people,
light, environment, the moment's realism). The bottom half re-reads
the photo — a sneaked-bite moment, travelers on a station platform,
someone zoning out over shaved ice, conversation at a food stall, or
just a memorable outline; everything else is stripped away; finally
only black hand-drawn lines plus a little ice-blue drawn from the
original photo shrink it into a tiny illustration with lots of
deliberate white space around it. The fun part: beyond turning the
photo into an illustration, it helps you re-decide what in this photo
is truly worth keeping. Full instructions are in the images / first
comment.
