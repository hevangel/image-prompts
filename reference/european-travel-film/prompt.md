## The prompt

The full prompt sits in the author's comment on both the set post and the reel. The creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives in that comment, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt turns each uploaded photograph — landscape and travel photos in particular — into its own vertical comparison poster, handling photos independently and in upload order, never combining photos or reusing another photo's subjects, scenery, colours or text:

- **Layout.** A strict 3:4 portrait canvas in exactly two equal horizontal halves (50% / 50%), directly joined at the midpoint, with no side-by-side layout, third section, border, divider, arrow, Before/After label, logo or watermark.
- **Upper half.** The uploaded original preserved faithfully — subjects, people, expressions, poses, buildings, landscape, roads, water, object positions, spatial relationships, lighting and composition unchanged; no repainting, regeneration, substitution or alteration of subjects.
- **Lower half — grading only, no re-rendering.** The same photograph, viewpoint, framing and scene as the foundation, re-graded with a Dazz analog-film feel and Lightroom-style colour grading toward a European vintage travel-editorial look. Grading adapts per photo: gently reduce excessive saturation; muted steel-blue/gray-blue skies where appropriate; authentic stone, brick, wood and metal colours preserved; greenery subdued without losing detail; realistic water reflections, warm sunset tones, natural skin colours and recognisable colours of vibrant subjects retained; no single orange-brown, golden or blue palette imposed on all images. Highlights and shadows refined with gentle cinematic contrast, detail and dimensionality retained, overly digital sharpness softened, subtle realistic fine film grain added. No exaggerated light leaks, scratches, timestamps, heavy filters, artificial colour casts or excessive vintage effects. All buildings, structures, people, animals, terrain, roads, waterways, sky composition, object counts, scales, positions, perspective and meaningful details are preserved in both halves; no invented clouds, weather changes, new light sources, replaced backgrounds or reconstructed scenery.
- **Text rules.** Restrained, elegant white English serif typography only in the lower half: the verified real city/region name in uppercase with generous letter spacing as the main title, and the verified attraction/landmark name as a smaller subtitle, placed in available negative space without obscuring important subjects. If a location is not supplied and cannot be reliably established, location text is omitted entirely rather than guessed.
- **Final check.** Strict equal 50/50 split, original on top, composition and subjects as consistent as possible, naturally graded vintage-film travel-editorial interpretation below, one refined poster per input photo.

## English translation of the introduction

Sharing a prompt that makes Taiwan attractions look like they have a European travel-film tone — very suitable for trying with landscape photos. The author says he likes this look but is not very good at adjusting light and colour mixing, so he turned the process into a simple prompt for everyone to try. The lower half simulates vintage-film colour and texture: vivid colours are toned down so sky, buildings and trees look calmer; bright areas are softened, shadows deepened, and a little fine film grain is added, plus simple English text, giving the feel of photos once seen in travel magazines. Because each original photo's colours differ, results will differ. The author notes these are AI-simulated grading effects, not photos retouched one by one in Lightroom, and a small number of details may differ from the original; the inspiration was shooting with a Dazz camera's film effect and then adjusting colour and light in Lightroom. Readers are invited to copy the prompt, post their results in the comments, and follow for the next set.

## How this relates to neighboring entries

Filed as its own entry: it joins the comparison-poster family (see `comparison-poster`, `fresh-illustration`, `color-block-breakout`, `japanese-retro-manga`, `abstract-gouache`, `neon-thick-impasto`, `japanese-hand-drawn`, `translucent-tracing-paper`, `white-space-illustration` and `black-white-drawing`) as its photographic branch — unlike the family's illustrated branches, the lower half here is not redrawn or repainted at all, only re-graded, and it is the family's first travel-photography grading style rather than an illustration style. It is also distinct from `mediterranean-poster` (an illustrated 1930s–60s travel poster) and from `retro-film` (a CMYK halftone editorial print).
