## The prompt

The source posts' author comment carries the creator's full GPT prompt
(in English; identical on set and reel). The creator publishes it for
followers to copy. The full text lives in that comment, linked above;
what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt asks for one independent high-end design poster per photo,
on a 3:4 canvas in two equal halves. The top half preserves the
original photograph faithfully with subtle refined grading. The bottom
half extracts only the most recognizable subject, contours, structure,
pose and narrative relationships and reconstructs them as an ancient
Chinese artifact ink rubbing / archaeological rubbing — never a
whole-photo copy, an ordinary black-and-white filter or a traced line
drawing: the subject is compressed to core forms like stone carvings,
shallow reliefs or ancient ornament and reinterpreted through
rubbing-transfer logic so the source photo stays instantly
recognizable. The model is explicitly given authority to redirect the
composition — rescue even poor photos by subtraction, rearrangement,
cropping, scale changes and whitespace; the subject may be incomplete,
off-center, edge-touching, floating or partly missing, kept small
against an exceptionally large expanse of deliberate whitespace. The
image is built from ink marks, blank areas and losses rather than
complete outlines — contours may break, disappear or vary; black areas
show missing ink, dry-brush white gaps, grain, erosion, fissures and
weathering from a time-worn artifact surface, while key structure
stays accurate. The palette is warm white / off-white / natural paper
plus deep ink black / gray-black, with almost no conventional color
or gradients; moderate paper fibers and old rubbing-paper grain,
avoiding excessive yellowing and cheap artificial aging — mottled
ink-transfer tonal layers, not a noise filter. Typography is minimal
and editorial, in any language: a few titles, notes or archival-style
words from the subject, location, culture or mood, placed quietly in
the whitespace or beside the rubbing's edges, in an
archaeological-catalog relationship. Excluded: whole-photo copying,
excess background, ordinary black-and-white line art, vector outlines,
noise filters, filling the frame, and template-based artificial aging.

## English translation of the introduction

The caption (Traditional Chinese; the set's and reel's are slightly
different edits of the same text) shares a photo-rubbing method that
recreates the true texture of characters, paper and ink: the top half
keeps the photo; the bottom half deletes excess background and detail
and rebuilds, keeping only people's interactions, postures, movements
and the most memorable relationships between objects, turning them
into ink traces as if rubbed from stone carvings, reliefs or ancient
artifacts — some lines break, some areas are deliberately left
uninked, with cracks, paper fibers and a great deal of surrounding
white space. An ordinary photo becomes a long-preserved "trace," and
the rubbing prompt greatly upgrades the image's texture. Readers are
invited to copy the prompt from the images / first comment, post their
results, share, and follow.
