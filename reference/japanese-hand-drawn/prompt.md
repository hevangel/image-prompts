## The prompt

The full prompt sits in the author's comment on both the set post and the reel. The creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives in that comment, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt turns each uploaded photograph into its own vertical comparison poster, handling photos independently — never mixing images, colors, subjects or compositions between photos:

- **Layout.** One vertical image: the original photograph above, the illustration below.
- **Illustration — simplification.** The scene is reduced to its main subject, action or defining feature — a look, a pose, a building outline, a distinctive small corner — and roughly 70% of the frame is left blank, on a lightly textured paper-like ground.
- **Illustration — linework.** Japanese minimalist manga editorial design with expressive hand-drawn ink: strokes vary in thickness, uneven, with dry-brush and broken passages as if drawn stroke by stroke, plus solid black blocks and manga halftone (screentone) dots that give the subject volume.
- **Illustration — color.** Black-and-white ink lines recombined with one or two of the source photo's most representative colors (e.g. blue-green for street dance, sky blue for extreme cycling, clay-orange for tennis).
- **Text rules.** No added titles, text, extra subjects or decorative elements. The prompt ends with a self-check against those rules.

## English translation of the introduction

The caption shares instructions for turning photos into Japanese hand-drawn line art, thanking Facebook friends for the instruction set and the idea. The method is: photo, then simplification, then a hand-drawn treatment. What the author loves most is the black ink lines — not neat and smooth, but varying in thickness, with dry-brush and broken strokes, as if drawn stroke by stroke — plus local black blocks and manga screentone dots that make the subject three-dimensional and manga-like. The style deliberately leaves about 70% blank, keeping only the most important person, action or feature, and uses one or two most representative colors taken from the original photo, recombined with black-and-white ink lines. Example photos are credited to Pexels.

## How this relates to neighboring entries

Filed as its own entry, not folded into `fine-line-poster` (簡單線條) or `one-line-contour` (只用幾條線): those are defined by uniformly fine, neat lines and by a single continuous contour respectively, while this style is defined by multiple hand-drawn ink strokes of varying thickness with dry-brush and broken passages, solid black blocks, manga halftone dots, about 70% white space, and one or two source-derived colors — a manga-editorial comparison look. It also joins the comparison-poster family (see `comparison-poster`, `fresh-illustration`, `color-block-breakout`, `japanese-retro-manga`, `abstract-gouache` and `neon-thick-impasto`) as its hand-drawn-lines branch.
