## The prompt

The source post's author comment carries the creator's full GPT prompt
(written in Chinese, with a Chinese introduction). The creator
publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives in that
comment, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt asks for a vertical 3:4 "before/after comparison" design
poster per photo — multiple photos become one independent poster each,
never combined — in two equal-height zones meeting naturally at the
center, with NO borders, divider lines, arrows, or Before/After
labels. The top zone is the original photo filling full width,
faithfully preserving the main people/animals/objects, poses, actions,
spatial relations, scene, natural light and main color atmosphere,
with only extremely subtle natural color adjustment; natural
proportions, no stretching or redesigning of main subjects; small
unimportant edges may be cropped, and if cropping would remove a main
subject, a little environment background may be extended naturally —
but never creating, replacing or modifying key people, objects,
scenes or actions not in the original. The bottom zone is a geometric
colored-paper abstraction (cut colored-paper collage look) from the
same photo only: not redrawing the whole photo or copying every
object/detail, but identifying the most worth-keeping recognizable
information — core subject, most recognizable contour,
pose/direction, interactions and main occlusion relations, a few
environment elements that represent the scene — deleting secondary
background, clutter and decoration, and keeping the core subject's
approximate position, direction, pose, movement and interactions so
top and bottom correspond at first glance (not pixel-perfect). The
subject may shrink moderately with generous negative space
(contemporary art book / independent publication / design journal
editorial feel). Figures, objects and space are built from a limited
number of polygons, simplified curves and flat color blocks — not
over-simplified into generic icons or symbols; structure and depth
come from adjacent flat color blocks of differing shape and value. No
gradients, thick black outlines, 3D shadows, realistic materials, 3D
modeling, cartoon faces, plastic feel, clashing neon, random doodles
or glitch art. Color: about 5–6 of the photo's most representative
colors extracted into a coordinated, slightly desaturated system; the
bottom background is warm off-white / ivory / pale beige; the darkest
color is reserved for a few key focal points; one main accent color
echoes the photo; the core subject's signature colors are never
changed. Edges are clean and sharp; a very subtle print grain inside
a few blocks and very slight misregistration at a few edges are
allowed for physical print texture — not rough paper, vintage dirt,
heavy grain or glitch. Typography is very sparse, with an
archival-number / art-publication feel in the bottom's negative space:
one short, natural, restrained English title auto-generated from the
photo, plus small numbering text, placed at different positions (e.g.
opposite diagonal corners), always in negative space, never covering
the main subject; no fabricated places, dates, identities or event
names, and no dense explanatory text. Overall: a page from a
contemporary art book / independent publication / high-end design
journal — restrained, quiet, gentle, clean, ordered, lots of
whitespace. Core principle: do not make the photo more complex and do
not just redraw it — organize a complex photo into fewer, simpler
geometric color blocks while staying instantly recognizable.

> Full prompt text: see the source post's author comment (linked above).

## English translation of the introduction

The caption (Traditional Chinese) shares a colored-paper collage
instruction: take a complex photo and have AI organize it into simple
colored paper, still recognizable as what was photographed. The photo
is not redrawn or copied in full — identify what is most important
(the most recognizable subject, the pose/contour/direction/color you
would remember at a glance). The top half keeps the original photo,
real and complete; the bottom half reorganizes the same photo into a
geometric color-block paper-style image, deleting unimportant
background detail while keeping key contours, position, pose and
color relations, converging a complex scene into a quiet moment of a
few color blocks — above, the real world; below, an organized visual
memory. It suits travel photos, street shots, people, pets,
architecture and everyday snaps, especially information-dense frames.
The full instruction is in the images / first comment.
