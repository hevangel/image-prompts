## The prompt

The full prompt sits in the post caption and identically in the author's comment on the set post. The creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives there, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt turns each uploaded photograph, separately and in upload order (never mixing subjects or elements between photos), into its own independent "Original Photography × Three-Color Reduction Linocut" comparison poster on a strict vertical 4:5 canvas, split horizontally into exactly two equal 50% halves, seamlessly joined — no borders, additional panels, captions, arrows, logos or watermarks:

- **Top half — original photography.** A faithful copy of the original photo — recognisable subjects, facial features where present, poses, architectural proportions, perspective, surroundings, authentic textures, natural lighting and original colours — with only minimal proportional cropping or natural peripheral background extension, no distortion, artistic effects or structural changes.
- **Bottom half — three-color reduction linocut.** The same complete scene reinterpreted as a handcrafted three-color reduction linocut print on pure-white archival paper, keeping the main subject's recognisable silhouette, proportions, pose, structural features and visual relationships. Only secondary background details may be simplified or subtly rearranged for balance, without inventing unrelated elements. Exactly three harmonious inks are extracted automatically from the source photograph: one dark ink for shadows and major forms, one midtone ink for supporting structures, and one restrained accent ink; untouched white paper serves as the negative space, with no fourth ink. Bold carved shapes, expressive irregular contours, rhythmic white cut marks, layered flat colours, subtle ink misregistration, uneven ink pressure, fine grain and authentic handmade print texture. Clothing, foliage, buildings, roads, ground, water and reflections are translated into simplified carved marks while the scene's identity and natural spatial logic are retained. No invented water, buildings or landmarks; tiny pedestrians only when naturally appropriate. If the city is confidently identifiable, its name appears in small uppercase sans-serif at the upper-left of the lower artwork; otherwise no text at all.
- **Avoid.** Photorealistic rendering, watercolor, glossy 3D, smooth vector perfection, gradients, extra colours, decorative typography and unrelated objects.
- **Priorities / final check.** A striking, elegant, collectible photographic-to-vintage-linocut transformation with exact 50:50 alignment and strong subject continuity.

## English translation of the introduction

What would everyday photos look like if they were redrawn using only a few colours? This time I put street cleaners, everyday city scenes, and even a large dam into three-colour reduction linocuts — and I didn't expect these ordinary scenes to come out with so much character and artistic flavour. While making these photo conversions I discovered something interesting: you don't necessarily need a beautiful landscape photo. Street cleaners, everyday work in the city, even large dam facilities can all become distinctive retro illustrations. What attracts me most about this style is the simple colour combinations, the rough hand-carved lines, plus a touch of irregular print grain, which give originally realistic photos a sense of nostalgia. Look closely and you'll see the figures' original movements, the buildings' structures, even the lines of flowing water are all preserved — just presented in a different way. And different photos come out with different colours and moods; each one has its own character. You don't need a magnificent landscape — ordinary everyday life also has scenes worth turning into artworks.

(Then the creator asks readers which conversion they like best, invites follows and shares, and invites readers to post their own creations made with the prompt in the comments.)
