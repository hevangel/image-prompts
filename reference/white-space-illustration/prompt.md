## The prompt

The full prompt sits in the author's comment on both the set post and the reel. The creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives in that comment, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt turns each uploaded photograph into its own vertical comparison poster, handling photos independently and in upload order — never collaging or mixing images, colors, subjects or compositions between photos:

- **Layout.** A strict 3:4 portrait canvas in exactly two equal horizontal halves: the original photograph above, the minimalist line illustration below, with no dividers, borders, labels, logos or watermarks.
- **Upper half.** Stays faithful to the original — the scene as photographed, with only minimal adjustment.
- **Lower half — reduction.** Complex backgrounds are stripped away; only the meaning worth preserving is kept — a small protagonist rendered as a conceptual line illustration, with expansive quiet negative space around it.
- **Lower half — medium and color.** Restrained light gray-blue line work on old-notebook-style paper, with a paper texture; the illustration keeps the feeling of the scene rather than its detail.
- **Text rules.** Short handwritten-style phrases from the scene may appear in the illustration (as in set photos 2 and 10); no invented places, dates or decorative microcopy.

> Full prompt text: see the source post's author comment (linked above).

## English translation of the introduction

Some scenes we pass every day, yet never stop to look at — a bicycle outside an old shop, sunlight shadows on a wall, a back bent over work in a night market. The introduction goes on to say that this photo white-space illustration prompt is being shared, that copying the instruction and uploading one's own photo allows a trial, and that readers are welcome to post their results and to share and follow for more free prompts.

## How this relates to neighboring entries

Filed as its own entry, not folded into `japanese-hand-drawn`: both are split posters with a line illustration below the photo, but this style's illustration is a restrained light gray-blue conceptual line drawing with a small protagonist in vast quiet space on notebook paper, while `japanese-hand-drawn` uses expressive manga ink with dry-brush and broken strokes, solid black blocks and halftone dots in one or two of the photo's own representative colors. It joins the comparison-poster family (see `comparison-poster`, `fresh-illustration`, `color-block-breakout`, `japanese-retro-manga`, `abstract-gouache`, `neon-thick-impasto`, `japanese-hand-drawn` and `translucent-tracing-paper`) as its white-space branch.
