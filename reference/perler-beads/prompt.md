## The prompt

The source set's author comment carries the creator's full GPT prompt
(written in Chinese, with a Chinese introduction). The creator
publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives in that
comment, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt asks for one complete left–right before/after design per
uploaded image — exactly two equal-width panels, strict 1:1, no
top/bottom split and no third area. The LEFT panel is the original
photo completely preserved and unmodified: no redrawing, retouching,
filters, recoloring, additions/removals or stretching; proportional
scaling with natural whitespace, never cropping the key subject, no AI
outpainting. The RIGHT panel redesigns the same photo as a Fuse Bead /
Perler Bead minimal editorial poster — explicitly not plain pixel art,
a pixel filter or a mosaic: the core subject, contour, proportions,
pose, viewpoint, main structure, spatial relations, mood and
representative colors are analyzed first; the subject's recognizable
contour, proportions, pose, direction, signature features and core
colors are preserved, simplifying only detail beads cannot render.
The subject is built from hundreds of identical cylindrical plastic
beads in a regular grid, each with a center hole, fine gaps, and matte
low-reflectance plastic; optionally one ring of off-white beads forms
a clean outline. The background is a clean warm low-saturation
cream/ivory matte surface with generous negative space, lit like
studio still-life product photography of a real finished bead piece
(soft light from upper left, slight floating shadow for thickness). A
COLOR CARD below the subject auto-extracts 3–5 actually-used
representative colors — same-size flat solid blocks, evenly spaced, no
gradient, 3D, outline or shadow. Typography: an auto-generated simple,
poetic Chinese main title (2–6 characters) plus a short subtitle (6–14
characters) in a thin high-contrast Song/Ming serif, centered in the
whitespace; high-end Chinese magazine / independent publication /
exhibition catalogue feel. The right panel's layout order is fixed:
large top whitespace → bead subject (about 35–50% of panel height,
complete, centered slightly above middle) → color blocks → main title
→ subtitle → large bottom whitespace; nothing crosses the midline and
the panels meet directly with no gap, border, divider, labels,
Before/After text, arrows, logos or watermarks. Excluded: modifying
the left photo, ordinary pixel art / mosaic / LEGO / glass beads /
cross-stitch / embroidery / knitting / felt / paper carving, a
right-side original background, rainbow palettes, invented colors,
gradient cards, cartoonization, identity or pose changes.

> Full prompt text: see the source set's author comment (linked above).

## English translation of the introduction

The caption (Traditional Chinese) says he recently played with photos
using a familiar thing — perler beads. Real beads are individual
plastic beads with small round holes, thickness and a touchable feel,
not a flat pixel grid. The left side fully preserves the original
photo; the right side first understands the photo's most important
things (poses, outlines, clothing colors, or the most recognizable
features of architecture, pets, food), strips excess background, and
re-assembles the memorable subject from beads. Up close you see each
bead's hole, arrangement, thickness and plastic texture; from afar it
is still the original photo. The AI also extracts 3–5 representative
colors as small color cards below the work, plus a simple Chinese
title. The result feels less like a children's craft and more like a
design magazine's color research / a small visual archive of life
fragments. People, pets, travel, architecture, food and street photos
all suit it.
