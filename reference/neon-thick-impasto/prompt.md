## The prompt

The full prompt sits in the author's comment on both the set post and the reel. The creator publishes it for followers to copy. The full text lives in that comment, linked above; what follows is a structural summary.

The prompt turns one uploaded photo into a vertical comparison poster:

- **Layout.** One vertical image, strict 9:16 aspect ratio, divided into two equal panels stacked vertically — top panel the original, bottom panel the transformed version — separated only by a thin white horizontal line.
- **Top panel.** The original photograph preserved faithfully, with only subtle exposure, contrast and white-balance correction; nothing stylized, retouched, added, removed, replaced or rearranged.
- **Bottom panel — fidelity.** The same crop as the top panel, keeping the same scene, composition, perspective, subject positions, object sizes and spatial relationships.
- **Bottom panel — medium.** Repainted as richly textured thick impasto acrylic/oil painting: visible palette-knife marks, broad flat-brush strokes, layered paint ridges, tactile canvas grain and occasional paint drips, with paint blocks appearing to protrude from the canvas. Skies carry swirling, flowing brush marks, buildings become thick blocks of color, and ground reflections read as paint pulled downward with a brush.
- **Bottom panel — color.** An expressive, highly saturated neon-leaning palette — magenta, hot pink, orange, electric blue, teal and violet, with restrained yellow highlights — with color placement following the original photograph. Sky, buildings and sea reflections render like neon lights, bright with a slight outward glow, set against deep blue and deep purple shadows so the bright colors stand out, as if the painting were faintly glowing. Softer traditional oil-painting tones are avoided.
- **Text rules.** No added borders (other than the thin divider), titles, labels, captions, logos, signatures or watermarks. Readable in-scene signage counts as part of the scene and is preserved.
- **Best subjects.** Recommended especially for sunset, night-scene and reflection photos, where the contrast is strongest.

## English translation of the introduction

The caption (Traditional Chinese) shares a way to turn photos into a heavy neon oil-painting look, suited to everyday landscape photos for their strong contrast. Unlike softer, finer instructions shared before, this one keeps the original on top and renders the bottom in thick, layered impasto, with vivid neon colors that make a familiar scene livelier and its light more present. Example photos are credited to Pexels.

## How this relates to neighboring entries

Filed as its own entry, not folded into `impasto-oil-painting`: that entry is also thick impasto, but in a vivid saturated palette against generous warm-white negative space as a standalone poster treatment, while this style is a comparison poster whose lower half uses a neon palette that glows against deep blue and deep purple shadows. It also joins the comparison-poster family (see `comparison-poster`, `fresh-illustration`, `color-block-breakout`, `japanese-retro-manga` and `abstract-gouache`) as its neon-impasto branch.
